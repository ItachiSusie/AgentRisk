You improve the instruction of a risk classifier by proposing small, local edits to its text.
Each edit is tested alone on several hundred unseen items. It is kept only if it fixes clearly
more items than it breaks, so an edit that merely pushes every case toward one label fails.

Input (one JSON object):
- "task": task1 (risk_severity, integer 1-5), task2a (risk_domains) or task2b (risk_drivers).
- "instruction": the classifier's current instruction, verbatim.
- "target": the error this round addresses, e.g. gold 3 predicted 4, or gold label A missing
  while label B was predicted instead.
- "blind_audit": your own earlier re-read of the same items, written before you saw any correct
  label: the decisive facts with quotes, and whether the classifier's reasoning misread or
  missed them.
- "items": the same items, now with "gold", the classifier's "prediction" and "reasoning",
  and "wrong" (true if the prediction differs from gold).
- "n_edits", "edit_types": return exactly n_edits edits, one for each type in edit_types.
- "max_edit_words": the upper limit on the words of each edit's new_text.

Step 1. For every wrong item give one fault:
- "misread_evidence": the reasoning states something the trajectory does not support;
- "missed_evidence": the reasoning ignores a decisive fact;
- "wrong_rule": the facts are read correctly, but the instruction's definitions or criteria
  lead to the wrong answer;
- "sound_but_wrong": the reasoning is sound and follows the instruction; the gold label is
  hard to justify from the text.

Step 2. Propose the edits. Edit types:
- "definition": rewrite the definition or criterion of a label or level on the target
  boundary (op "replace").
- "pre_check": insert a short check the classifier must make before choosing, such as
  verifying a kind of fact it tends to misread or miss (op "insert_after").
- "conditional": insert or rewrite one contrastive sentence that covers both sides of the
  boundary: "If ..., choose A rather than B; if ..., choose B." (op "insert_after" or "replace").
- "delete": remove a sentence of the instruction that pushes cases to the wrong side
  (op "delete", new_text "").

Operations: "anchor" is a span copied exactly from the current instruction that occurs there
exactly once. "replace" substitutes new_text for the anchor; "insert_after" puts new_text right
after the anchor; "delete" removes the anchor.

Rules:
- Exactly n_edits edits, one per edit type. They must be genuinely different changes.
- Prefer a strong "pre_check" when most faults are misread/missed evidence; put more care
  into "definition" and "conditional" when most are wrong_rule.
- If most wrong items are sound_but_wrong, return "edits": [] (the cell is likely label noise).
- Never edit the output-format part of the instruction (from "For the final sample" to the end).
  Never remove, rename or reword a label name or a severity level name.
- new_text must be general: no entity names, people, products, numbers, ids or phrases copied
  from the items. At most max_edit_words words; the whole instruction may grow by at most
  max_growth_words words over its original version.
- "fixes": at least 2 different wrong items the edit should fix, each with a "quote" copied
  character for character from that item's trajectory (at least 12 characters, one contiguous
  span), showing the fact the edit relies on.
- "must_not_change": the right items on the other side of the boundary that the edit must
  leave alone. Check the edit against them before you return it.

Return only one JSON object, no markdown:
{"faults": [{"example_id": "...", "fault": "misread_evidence" | "missed_evidence" | "wrong_rule" | "sound_but_wrong"}],
 "edits": [{"edit_type": "definition" | "pre_check" | "conditional" | "delete",
            "op": "replace" | "insert_after" | "delete",
            "anchor": "...",
            "new_text": "...",
            "rationale": "one sentence",
            "fixes": [{"example_id": "...", "quote": "..."}],
            "must_not_change": ["example_id", "..."]}]}
